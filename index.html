Who We Are

A South Sudanese organisation, working with communities for their own future.

Mission Empowering Africa (MEA) is a National Organisation registered by the Relief and Rehabilitation Commission under the NGO Act of 2016, Registration No. 3676. Our Head office is in Juba and we work in communities across South Sudan and beyond.

Our mandate is to empower children, women and youth through education and income-generating skills, so that their families can stand on their own over time. We do not arrive with ready answers. We sit with people, listen, and build together.

Our Vision

A peaceful, prosperous and healthy world where everyone can shape their own life.

Our Mission

Give communities a platform to share information, mobilize resources and ask for what they need.

Our Mandate

Empower children, women and youth through education and income-generating skills.

Our Approach

Dialogue, media and community, three ways to build lasting peace.

How We Work

Three ways we build peace.

Differences are part of life. Violence is not. We move communities away from confrontation and toward solutions they can own. This works at every level, from a family dispute up to a county negotiation.

01

Dialogue

We bring people together across dividing lines, including those with power and those who rarely get a platform, to find goals they can actually share. Often this means children, women, youth and local leaders sitting in the same room.

  • Mediation between communities
  • Training of local activists
  • Quiet back channel conversations
02

Media

Radio, television and social platforms give people a way to talk about the real causes of violence and what it would take to move past them. Conversations that start on air often continue in the market the next morning.

  • TV and radio call in shows
  • Live streaming campaigns
  • Facebook, Twitter and community pages
03

Community

The hardest work happens close to home. We give neighbours, families and divided groups a safe space to sort things out. Sometimes that is under a tree, sometimes on a football pitch.

  • Under the tree dialogues
  • Shared farming projects
  • Soccer, athletics and wrestling
  • Public debates and conferences

These three approaches run across all four of our thematic areas. A women's savings group is also a dialogue space. A football match is also a peacebuilding tool.

What does MEA actually do?
We work in four areas: education and advocacy, peacebuilding and reconciliation, youth and women empowerment, and food security and livelihoods. On the ground this looks like community dialogues, training of local activists, women's savings groups, radio call in shows, shared farms, and sports and public debates. Everything is done together with the community.
Where does MEA work?
Our headquarters is in Juba, Central Equatoria. From there we run programmes in several states and counties across South Sudan, depending on where communities have invited us and where the need is greatest. If you want to know whether we are active in your area, get in touch.
How is MEA registered?
We are legally registered by the Relief and Rehabilitation Commission under Chapter 3, Section 10 of the NGO Act of 2016. Our registration number is 3676. We work within South Sudanese law and follow all child protection and safeguarding requirements.
How can I support MEA?
Three ways mainly. You can give, either financially or in kind, such as seeds, tools, school materials or equipment. You can volunteer your time and skills. Or you can partner with us as an organisation, whether that means co-funding a programme or bringing technical expertise. Reach out and we will find the right fit.
How are donations used?
Donations go into programme delivery: training materials, seeds and tools, transport to reach remote communities, radio airtime, and the staff who run the work on the ground. We keep overheads low and can share a breakdown of spending with any donor who asks.
Can my organisation partner with MEA?
Yes. We partner with NGOs, community based organisations, government bodies, faith groups and private companies. If you work in any of our four thematic areas, or you have resources that could support them, we would like to talk. Write to us with a short note about your organisation and what you have in mind.
Do you work with local leaders and government?
Always. Nothing we do works without the buy in of local leaders, elders, women's representatives and youth. We also work with county and state authorities where it helps the work move forward. Community ownership is central. If a programme only belongs to us, it will not outlast us.
Can I visit a project site?
Yes, with advance notice. We ask visitors to coordinate with us first so we can arrange transport, inform the community, and make sure your visit does not disrupt an ongoing activity. Donors and partners are welcome to see the work first hand.
