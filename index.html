Who We Are

A Platform Built With Communities

Mission Empowering Africa (MEA) is a South Sudanese organisation working for gender equality and the rights of every woman, girl, boy and man. We are legally registered by the Relief and Rehabilitation Commission under Chapter 3, Section 10 of the NGO Act, 2016 — Registration No. 3676 — with our headquarters in Juba.

Our mandate is to empower children, women and youth through education and income-generating skills, so that livelihoods can hold up on their own. We don't parachute in. We sit under the tree, in the market, in the classroom — and we build from there.

Our Vision

A peaceful, prosperous, healthy world where everyone has the power to shape their own life.

Our Mission

Give communities a platform to share information, mobilize resources, and push for what they need.

Our Mandate

Empower children, women and youth through education and income-generating skills.

Our Approach

Dialogue, media and community — three ways to build lasting peace.

How We Work

Three Ways We Build Peace

Conflict and differences are inevitable. Violence is not. We move communities away from confrontation and towards cooperative solutions — at every level, from a family dispute to a national negotiation.

01

Dialogue

We bring people together across dividing lines — those in power and those without a platform — to find goals they can actually share. Often that means children, women, youth and local leaders in the same room.

  • Mediation between communities
  • Training of activists
  • Back-channel diplomacy
02

Media

We use radio, TV and social platforms to get people talking about the real causes of violence — and about what it would take to move past them. Conversations that start on air often continue in the market.

  • TV & radio call-in shows
  • Live streaming campaigns
  • Facebook, Twitter & social media
03

Community

The hardest work happens close to home. We give neighbours, families and divided groups a safe space to work things out — often under a tree, sometimes on a football pitch.

  • Under-the-tree dialogues
  • Shared farming projects
  • Soccer, athletics & wrestling
  • Public debates & conferences

These three approaches run across all four of our thematic areas. A women's savings group is also a dialogue space. A football match is also a peacebuilding tool.

What does MEA actually do?
We work in four areas: education and advocacy, peacebuilding and reconciliation, youth and women empowerment, and food security and livelihoods. We run community dialogues, train activists, support women's savings groups, run radio call-in shows, set up shared farms, and organise sports and public debates. Everything we do is done with the community, not handed down to them.
Where does MEA work?
Our headquarters are in Juba, Central Equatoria. From there we run programmes in several states and counties across South Sudan, depending on where communities have invited us and where the need is greatest. If you want to know whether we're active in your area, get in touch.
How is MEA registered?
We are legally registered by the Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC) under Chapter 3, Section 10 of the NGO Act, 2016. Our registration number is 3676. We operate fully within South Sudanese law and follow all child protection and safeguarding requirements.
How can I support MEA?
Three ways, mainly. You can give — financially or in kind (seeds, tools, school materials, equipment). You can volunteer your time and skills. Or you can partner with us institutionally, whether that's co-funding a programme or bringing technical expertise. Reach out and we'll find the right fit.
How are donations used?
Donations go into programme delivery — training materials, seeds and tools, transport to reach remote communities, radio airtime, and staff who run the work on the ground. We keep overheads low and can share a breakdown of spending with any donor who asks.
Can my organisation partner with MEA?
Yes. We partner with NGOs, community-based organisations, government bodies, faith groups and private companies. If you work in any of our four thematic areas, or you have resources that could support them, we'd like to talk. Write to us with a short note about your organisation and what you have in mind.
Do you work with local leaders and government?
Always. Nothing we do works without the buy-in of local leaders, elders, women's representatives and youth. We also work with county and state authorities where it helps the work move forward. Community ownership is the whole point — if it only belongs to us, it won't outlast us.
Can I visit a project site?
Yes, with advance notice. We ask visitors to coordinate with us first so we can arrange transport, inform the community, and make sure your visit doesn't disrupt an ongoing activity. Donors and partners are welcome to see the work first-hand.
